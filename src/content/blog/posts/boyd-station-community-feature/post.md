Boyd's Station is not a separate town so much as a project rooted in Cynthiana, Ky., where an arts and journalism residency draws photographers, writers and community storytellers into Harrison County. Over three days in June 2025, the work moved through storefronts, farms, kitchens, roadside repairs and a late-night set at the Aeolian Music Hall.

A Monday evening in early June opened along a wildflower meadow on the edge of town. White and pink blossoms filled the foreground, and the hills softened green behind them. Two men were already down by the water, one threading a fly line with the focused quiet of long habit.

![Wildflowers bloom across a hillside meadow in Cynthiana, Ky., June 9, 2025.](images/meadow.jpg "Wildflowers bloom across a hillside meadow in Cynthiana, Ky., June 9, 2025. (Photo by Caleb McCartney)")

By nightfall the kids on the property had found sparklers. A boy in a blue t-shirt swung one in a wide arc, smoke drifting into the dark around him, the moon faint above the tree line. An adult stood close enough to steady the moment. Nothing about it felt staged.

The next morning, the main strip carried the slower rhythm of a small Kentucky town. One shop window read, in clean white block letters: A BEAUTIFUL PLACE TO BE. Inside another, a woman with her arms crossed smiled across a glass case packed with ornate lamps, gilded vases and framed portraits. Artisan soaps, tea tree, peppermint, sat in damask-wrapped rows near the door.

![A shopkeeper stands among antiques and artwork inside a shop in Cynthiana, Ky., June 10, 2025.](images/shopkeeper.jpg "A shopkeeper stands among antiques and framed artwork in Cynthiana, Ky., June 10, 2025. (Photo by Caleb McCartney)")

Down the block, a turquoise vintage truck sat half-dissected at the curb. A man in sunglasses was stretched under the steering column. A woman with wavy blond hair and aviator glasses sat in the passenger seat watching him work. They had been at it for hours, she said. They would be at it for more.

> It is a beautiful place to be. The sign in the window does not lie.

On the second afternoon, coverage moved to a working farm on the edge of town. A man in a gray shirt and white cap moved along a row of vegetable seedlings, crouching to press a stem gently back into the soil. Two ranchers in matching cowboy hats stood at the pasture fence watching cattle graze. No one seemed interested in rushing the day along.

![A farmer tends seedlings in a vegetable garden in Cynthiana, Ky., June 10, 2025.](images/farmer.jpg "A farmer tends vegetable seedlings at dusk in Cynthiana, Ky., June 10, 2025. (Photo by Caleb McCartney)")

Thursday night the Aeolian Music Hall filled up. The red neon sign above the stage threw everything in warm light. A woman in a floral dress and wide-brimmed tan hat took the left stool. A man in a white shirt and red cowboy hat settled at center with an acoustic guitar. A third musician, bearded and dressed in black, took the right. They played for two hours to a room that did not want to leave.

![Three musicians perform at the Aeolian Music Hall in Cynthiana, Ky., June 12, 2025.](images/music-hall.jpg "A trio performs at the Aeolian Music Hall in Cynthiana, Ky., June 12, 2025. (Photo by Caleb McCartney)")

By the end of the third day, the work was less about identifying a single place called Boyd Station than about seeing how Boyd's Station connected photographers, hosts and neighbors across Cynthiana. The photographs hold that network in small pieces: a meadow, a shop window, a garden row, a truck dashboard and a stage lit red after dark.
